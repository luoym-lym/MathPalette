Based on geometric principles, the shortest distance between two points is a straight line.

Let's analyze the paths:
*   **Route A** follows a path that goes up and down. The red segment rises 2 units over a horizontal distance of 2 units.
*   **Route B** has a red segment that rises 2 units over a horizontal distance of 2 units (following the grid diagonal).
*   **Route C** has a red segment that rises 2 units over a horizontal distance of 3 units.
*   **Route D** has a red segment that rises 2 units over a horizontal distance of 6 units (or possibly 1 unit, but visually much flatter than A and B).

In all cases, the total horizontal distance is the same (let's call it $W$) and the total vertical distance is the same (let's call it $H$). A direct straight line from start to finish would be the shortest. However, none of the drawn routes are a single straight line; they are all zig-zags composed of diagonal segments.

The length of a diagonal segment is determined by the formula $\sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$.
*   In **Route B**, the "steps" of the zig-zag are perfectly diagonal (moving 1 unit right and 1 unit up/down for each grid intersection). This corresponds to a slope of 1, which is the most efficient way to gain height horizontally on a square grid. The length of the path is exactly $\sqrt{2}$ times the grid width.
*   In **Route A**, the zig-zag segments are flatter (moving 2 units right for every 2 units up... wait, looking closely at A, the red line goes 2 units right and 2 units up, which is also a 1:1 ratio). Let's re-examine.
    *   **A:** Red segment goes 2 right, 2 up. Black segment goes 2 right, 2 down. The peak is at the center. Total length = $2\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2}$? No, that's the full width. If width is 4, A is two segments of length $\sqrt{2^2+2^2} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$. Total = $4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.66$.
    *   **B:** Red segment goes 2 right, 2 up. Then there is a small down-up-down-up pattern. Actually, looking at B, it has 4 segments. Each segment seems to go 1 right and 1 up/down. The total width is 4 units. Length = $4 \times \sqrt{1^2+1^2} = 4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.66$.
    *   **C:** The zig-zag has a lower amplitude and wider segments.
    *   **D:** The zig-zag is very tight.

Let's look closer at the geometry relative to the grid.
The length of a path that traverses a rectangle of width $W$ and height $H$ is minimized when the path is as close to a straight line as possible. Among zig-zag paths made of segments with a slope magnitude $|m|$, the length is generally minimized when the slope is closest to the slope of the ideal straight line.

However, let's look at the "triangle inequality" logic.
Compare A and B.
Route A goes straight up the diagonal for 2 units, then straight down. It touches the "ceiling" once.
Route B goes up, down, up, down. It touches the ceiling multiple times.
Usually, deviating from the straight line (going up and down) adds length. Route A deviates from the centerline the *least*. The peak of A is the midpoint. The paths in B, C, and D oscillate around the centerline more frequently.
The oscillations in B, C, and D represent additional distance traveled away from the direct path and back. For instance, going up and down a small "tooth" adds length compared to just going straight along the average height.

**Therefore, Route A is the shortest** because it has the fewest oscillations (changes in direction) and stays closest to the hypothetical straight line connecting the start and end points.
